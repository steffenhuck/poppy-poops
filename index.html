How puppies learn

  • You steer. Tap anywhere and she runs there and waits a moment. On a computer, the arrow keys work too.
  • Know when it's coming. Puppies need to go right after waking up, after playing, and a little while after eating. And roughly every hour in between.
  • Watch for the signs. Sniffing the ground, walking in circles, sitting at the door.
  • Treat right away, but not too early. The moment she's finished, in the right spot. Too early and she jumps up halfway through; a few seconds late and the treat is for something else.
  • Keep her spot boring. Cats and butterflies ruin everything: shoo them away. And don't take her out for nothing too often, or her spot turns into a playground.
  • The old way: "No!" She won't learn "not on the rug". She'll learn "not where you can see me", start hiding, and forget a lesson.
  • Clean every accident. If it still smells like a toilet, she'll use it as one.
  • Ask with a bell (in the house): a bell on the door rings every time she goes out. Soon she rings it herself when she needs to go.
  • The crate (in the house): puppies hate to soil their bed, so she'll hold on in there. Not forever, though.

Every well-timed treat in the right spot is a lesson (a paw print at the top). Once all paws are filled, she's learned it. A whole day takes two minutes, and the week has three days, in order. What she learns, and what she doesn't, carries over to the next day.
